How to Choose an Instant-Read Meat Thermometer

Photo: Pexels

An instant-read thermometer has a thin metal probe. You push it into the thickest part of the meat and it shows the inside temperature in seconds. No more cutting meat open to guess if it's done.

See instant-read thermometers on Amazon

Who it's good for

  • New cooks who aren't sure when meat is done.
  • Anyone who grills, roasts or smokes meat.
  • People who want juicy meat, not dry and overcooked.

What to look for

1. Reading speed

Faster readings mean less time with the oven or grill open. Check the product page for how many seconds it takes.

2. Easy-to-read display

A backlit or rotating display helps when you're grilling at night or reading from an angle.

3. Probe length

A longer probe keeps your hand farther from the heat and reaches the middle of thick roasts.

4. Water resistance

Kitchens get messy. A water-resistant thermometer is easier to wipe clean. Never put the electronics in the dishwasher unless the product page says you can.

Safe minimum temperatures (USDA)

  • Poultry (chicken, turkey): 165°F
  • Ground meats (beef, pork): 160°F
  • Beef, pork, veal, lamb steaks, chops and roasts: 145°F, then rest 3 minutes
  • Fish: 145°F

Always check the latest guidance at FoodSafety.gov.

Tip: Put the probe in the thickest part of the meat and keep it away from bones. Bones can give a wrong reading.
